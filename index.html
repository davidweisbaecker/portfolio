Business Case · E-Commerce

€100,000 in two months

In 2019 I built a dropshipping brand for sustainable kitchenware. One product, silicone lids that replace cling film, took off: €100,000 in revenue in November and December, at a return on ad spend of 3 to 4.

Dropshipping was the cheap way to test demand. The real work began once a product proved itself.

E-Commerce Performance Marketing Operations Shopify
  1. 01

    Finding the product

    I tracked which Facebook and Instagram ads gained views fastest. The first two products flopped; the third, silicone lids, took off. Beeswax wraps and silicone bags never got ads of their own but sold as add-ons.

  2. 02

    Store and ads

    Two Shopify stores, in English and German, with a fast theme, photo reviews, bundles and upsells. I produced the video ads myself and scaled Meta spend to €1,000–1,500 a day.

  3. 03

    When it scaled

    Growth broke the operation, not the ads. Orders moved to a sourcing agent in Shenzhen with about seven days' delivery, support went to email templates and student friends, and a private loan bridged PayPal holding back up to 30% of revenue.

Looking back

No later product broke even, and selling what Amazon offered cheaper and faster wasn't worth building on, so I stopped in 2021. Today I would test the add-ons as products of their own, keep the list of past buyers working with seasonal offers, run split tests even when things work, and let AI agents take routine support and a daily ad report.

Business Case · Coaching

A coaching business from zero

From 2021 to 2025 I ran Soul and Purpose: a 15-week one-to-one program, priced from €2,500 up to €4,000. About 30 clients went through it, up to seven at a time, partly while I lived in South America and Southeast Asia.

Several hundred discovery calls taught me the core of product work: find the need behind what people say.

Customer Discovery Offer & Pricing Sales Funnel Remote
  1. 01

    The offer

    15 weeks at one fixed price: a weekly one-to-one call, material to work through and one small habit a week. Clients rated twelve areas of life at the start and the end. Twice I also mentored new coaches on positioning, offer, pricing, website and funnel.

  2. 02

    The funnel

    Instagram content, direct messages, a free first call, then a sales call I closed myself. Paid Meta ads brought more calls but worse fits, so I stopped them. Referrals never came; few people talk openly about this topic.

  3. 03

    Remote and results

    Half a year in South America with calls from 8 a.m., half a year in Southeast Asia with calls late into the night. About 30 clients, one renewal, many positive Trustpilot reviews and video testimonials from six clients.

Team Project · In Progress

A nudge you can wear

In our Human–Machine Interface Design course at THI, my team is designing Align.: a necklace that notices when you've sat in the same position for too long and reminds you with a gentle, wave-like vibration. The companion app shows how you've been sitting, with a Duolingo-style avatar.

Get the flow right before you polish the pixels.

User Interviews Paper Prototyping Wearables Team Project
  1. 01

    Interviews

    We interviewed people who sit most of the day. The office worker I talked to sits about two hours at a stretch, gave up on his standing desk without knowing why and keeps notifications off at work. He wanted to snooze or skip a reminder right on the necklace, for example during a client call.

  2. 02

    Paper prototype

    We printed every screen of the app, laid out each transition as a paper arrow and walked through the whole flow with our professor. Gaps and dead ends showed up before anyone opened Figma.

  3. 03

    Next

    Usability tests, then the final concept, presented to companies in January 2027.

Design Experiment

From maker to curator

For my Gestaltung II course I worked on Neuland Next — the student-built app used across THI. Icons and screens I designed twice, fully by hand and fully with AI. For the data poster I picked the method myself, based on what the first two had shown.

AI delivers the structure in a fraction of the time. Consistency and quality still come from the designer — the future is hybrid.

AI vs. Manual UI & Icon Design Data Visualisation Python
  1. 01

    Icon set

    Eleven app icons, twice: vectorised by hand in Illustrator (neumorphic) and generated with Gemini (skeuomorphic). AI cut the time to about a fifth — but it generates far better than it fine-tunes. Often a fresh prompt beats forcing a small detail.

  2. 02

    Screens

    Four app screens: built component by component in Figma, then generated with Gemini and refined in Photoshop. Screen by screen, the AI lost the visual thread — asking for all four in one single image forced consistency. That workaround was the breakthrough.

  3. 03

    Data poster

    Here the method was my call — and the first two experiments made it: AI. I analysed 21.2 million rows of app data with Python and Pandas, had Claude write the layout as SVG code and finished it print-ready in Illustrator. The finding: 14,266 timetable checks between 2 and 4 a.m.

Side Project · AI Agents

Agents on the night shift

Since October 2026, two agents I built with Claude run on a schedule: one turns my lecture slides into exercise booklets every night, the other checks career pages for product internships every Monday.

Writing an agent's brief is product work: define the output, set the limits, check the results.

AI Agents Prompt Design Automation Claude
  1. 01

    The brief

    Each agent has a written brief, like a product requirement: inputs, the exact output format, a budget per run and hard limits. When the output misses, I fix the brief.

  2. 02

    Study agent

    Every night it takes one lecture script from Google Drive and writes a printable exercise booklet: a summary with slide references, 12–25 exercises on three levels, a 30-minute mini exam and Anki flashcards. It sticks to the script and marks anything it adds.

  3. 03

    Internship scout

    Every Monday it checks dozens of career pages and job boards against my criteria and sends a short report. It only reports: it never applies or contacts anyone, and a role without a link doesn't count.

Product check

I also looked at turning the study agent into an app for other students. Two things argue against building right away: at an estimated €0.50–3 in AI costs per booklet, a flat €5 subscription doesn't hold up for heavy users, and NotebookLM already covers much of it for free. The next step would be a test with classmates, not an app.

Research Deep-Dive

Designing for the unpredictable

A seminar talk on the 18 Guidelines for Human–AI Interaction (Amershi et al., Microsoft Research, CHI 2019) — extended into how we instruct AI today.

Good AI design isn't about more AI — it's about giving people clarity, correction and control.

Human–AI Interaction UX Research AI Guardrails Workshop Design
  1. 01

    The paper

    Why AI breaks classic usability: it's unpredictable, makes mistakes and acts in the background — so trust erodes. The authors condensed 150+ recommendations into 18 guidelines and tested them with 49 HCI practitioners on 20 AI products.

  2. 02

    My extension

    The paper defines how AI should behave — not how to write that down. My discussion carries it into today's practice, where guidelines like G1, G6 and G11 end up in markdown system prompts, and shows where those instructions break: a single role-play reframing bypasses a robot's safety rules.

  3. 03

    The workshop

    The talk ends hands-on: in four groups, the audience drafts the rule file for an AI that reads X-rays — its limits, how it signals confidence and explains itself, how it avoids bias, when it hands over to a doctor, and how it treats health data.
